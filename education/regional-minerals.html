Guide 03 · Place

South Okanagan & Similkameen stones

Volcanic ash, old riverbeds, and metamorphic heat. The region writes in iron, silica, and contact textures.

Signature local stones

Red and polychrome jaspers — Similkameen corridor

Formed through silica-enrichment of volcanic ash or sediment. Color comes from iron-oxide inclusions. Look for a river-tumbled “skin” and a body that holds up under the lapidary saw.

Okanagan golden and yellow jasper — Oliver / Hedley

Often found near old mine workings. The yellow and gold tones come from limonite staining inside microcrystalline quartz.

Chalcedony, chert, and agate

Same family, different structure. Agate shows banding. Chalcedony offers smooth translucency. Chert stays largely opaque, in nodules or veins.

Altered skarns, argillite, and schist

These are contact stories: hot magmatic fluids meeting sedimentary rock. Expect dense assemblages and textures that do not look like river jasper at all.
