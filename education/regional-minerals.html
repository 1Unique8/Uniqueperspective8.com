Regional species profiles

Dedicated pages now carry the two silica families the shop is asked about most:

  • Similkameen jasper guide
  • BC agate & chalcedony guide

Red & polychrome jasper

Microcrystalline silica colored by iron oxides. Conchoidal fracture. Mohs 6.5–7.0. Polychrome varieties swirl red, brown, and tan.

Okanagan golden & yellow jasper

Limonite infusions in a cryptocrystalline quartz matrix. Found as float near older workings in Oliver / Hedley country — not as a labelled mine product.

Chalcedony & agate

Fibrous quartz with waxy luster and translucent edges. Agates are the banded members. If an LED finds no light in the margin, ask whether you are holding siltstone.

Black chert, argillite & siltstone

Fine-grained, often dark, generally opaque. They lack the waxy luster of chalcedony. Cabinet: UP8-PEN-005.

Optical calcite / Iceland spar

Rhombohedral cleavage, double refraction, Mohs 3, vigorous fizz. Do not confuse softness with quartz.

Galena & sulfide ores

Metallic luster, high density, cubic cleavage. Treat sulfides as specimens first, ore second.

Altered skarns, argillite, and schist

Contact stories. Dense textures that do not look like river jasper. Banded quartzite and gneiss from the Penticton cut sit in the cabinet as teaching stones.

Questions a collector actually asks

How do you tell Similkameen jasper from black chert?

Both sit near Mohs 6.5–7. Jasper usually carries iron-oxide color. Chert is typically darker and duller. Put an LED on a thin margin before you argue about the name.

Is every red stone from the Similkameen jasper?

No. If a knife bites it, if it fizzes, or if the edge never wakes under light, it is not jasper.

What may a provenance card say in public?

Roadcut or river system, date, collector, and FMC 292907 when that standing applied. Not a vault name, not a MINFILE occurrence, not “from the mine.” Read Provenance.

Can I collect anywhere in the South Okanagan?

No. Check tenure, private land, parks, and riparian setbacks. Start at ethical sourcing.
