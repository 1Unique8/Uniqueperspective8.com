Guide 01 · Law & stewardship

The BC Free Miner & ethical field code

British Columbia collecting is a balance of statute and manners. Know which permission you actually hold before the first test hole.

Two kinds of collecting

Before you step onto the ground, separate personal curiosity from commercial intent.

  • Recreational rockhounding is generally small amounts of rock and mineral for personal use. It does not cover mechanical equipment or the sale of what you find.
  • Commercial collecting and the Free Miner Certificate (FMC) is the standing required to prospect for minerals with intent to sell or to hold title. A valid British Columbia Free Miner Certificate grants specific rights to explore Crown land for minerals. It is not a mine permit and not a claim.

Morgan holds an FMC. That fact belongs on a provenance card. It does not travel onto every object in a shop listing. The studio standard is written on the Ethics page.

Claim verification

Respecting existing tenures is the first ethical act in the field.

  • Mineral Titles Online (MTO) is how we check land status: open Crown land, an active mineral claim held by someone else, or private property.
  • Permission is required before entering private land or an active claim. “It looked empty” is not a title search.

Cultural and environmental respect

This work takes place within the traditional, unceded territory of the Syilx Okanagan Nation.

  • BC Heritage Conservation Act: cultural artifacts and paleontological sites (fossils) stay in place. Do not collect them. Do not “tidy” them.
  • Leave No Trace: pack out waste, backfill test holes so they do not become erosion scars, and keep off sensitive riparian ground along the rivers.
