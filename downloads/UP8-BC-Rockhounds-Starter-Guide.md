A practical guide to ethical mineral collecting, identification, and field etiquette in the Southern Interior.

The Southern Interior of British Columbia offers a diverse geological landscape for the aspiring rockhound. From ancient volcanic flows to rich alluvial deposits, the South Okanagan and Similkameen regions provide accessible opportunities for collecting unique mineral specimens. This guide highlights five key areas to begin your journey, emphasizing safety, legality, and respect for the land.

## 1. Regional exploration: five accessible sites

| Site location | Target minerals | Access & conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Similkameen River gravel bars (Keremeos / Princeton corridor) | River-tumbled red jasper, polychrome chert, and dark river stones. | Easy shoreline access via public river access points. |
| Okanagan River alluvial corridor (Oliver area) | Golden-yellow jasper, quartz river cobbles, and fine river-rounded quartz veining. | Accessible via river pathways and public banks. |
| Yellow Lake scree & float (Highway 3A corridor) | Fine-grained volcanic rocks, chalcedony seams, and agates. | Found in weathered talus slopes alongside the highway corridor. |
| Hedley historical skarn float (Hedley / Nickel Plate road cuts) | Heavy mineralization, limestone/calcite matrices, garnet/skarn float, and pyritic veins. | Observation & float only; stay off active private mine tenures. |
| Sunday Summit gravels & roadside float (Highway 3 Pass) | Dark volcanic stones, porphyries, spiderweb vitrophyre, and microcrystalline silica. | Roadside exploration within the high mountain pass. |

## 2. Quick field identification checklist

- **The Wet Test.** Use a water spray bottle to douse your specimen. Water temporarily simulates a polish, revealing the true depth of color, hidden banding, and internal patterns that are often obscured by dust and oxidation.
- **The Knife Test.** Use a steel pocket knife to test hardness. If the blade scratches the rock, it is likely calcite or limestone (Mohs 3–4). If the blade slides smoothly off without leaving a mark, you have found harder silica-based minerals like jasper or quartz (Mohs 6.5–7).
- **The Flashlight Edge Test.** Hold a strong light source against the edge of the stone. Chalcedony and agates will exhibit a translucent glow, whereas chert and jasper remain opaque.

## 3. Ethical field code & safety essentials

### Land & legal verification

- **Land check.** Always verify open Crown land via Mineral Titles Online (MTO). It is illegal to collect on active mineral claims or private property without explicit permission.
- **Cultural respect.** Acknowledge that you are on the traditional, ancestral, and unceded territory of the Syilx Okanagan Nation. Leave historical or archaeological artifacts completely untouched; these are protected under the BC Heritage Conservation Act.

### Field etiquette

- **Leave no trace.** Pack out all trash, including any waste you find left by others.
- **Environmental protection.** Avoid digging into unstable riverbanks to prevent erosion and backfill any hand test holes before leaving the site.

### Essential gear list

- 10x loupe for close inspection
- Safety glasses (critical when using hammers)
- Durable leather gloves
- 22oz rock hammer
- Refillable water bottle
- Sturdy, high-traction boots
